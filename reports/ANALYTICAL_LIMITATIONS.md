# Analytical limitations and interpretation rules

1. **Estimated, partial rider coverage.** Customer journey measures are modeled estimates of unlinked train trips on weekdays from 6 AM to 11 PM. They exclude weekends and late nights and do not represent complete journeys with transfers. Net additional passenger minutes are therefore a measured-scope estimate, not every subway rider's total lost time.
2. **Signed time.** ATT and AJT can be negative. Net passenger minutes combine faster and slower trips at the aggregate level; they are not the sum of positive delays experienced by individual riders.
3. **Different source clocks.** Weekday incidents cover the full weekday; customer metrics cover 6 AM–11 PM. Service Delivered measures selected peak timepoints. Monthly associations have matching line/month labels but do not establish that the measured events affected the same passengers or hours.
4. **Unequal line definitions.** Incident tables combine N and W under N; customer and service tables separate them. The combined `NW` comparison group is appropriate only for cross-source analysis. The 42nd Street Shuttle is absent in customer data for January–February 2022. Service Delivered shuttle codes change in September 2024 and are mapped using the MTA GTFS route file.
5. **Missing versus zero.** April 2025 has customer metrics but no 42nd Street Shuttle delay-incident row. Major Incidents has a sparse positive-event structure, and eight events have no assigned line. The monthly view retains nulls for missing cells and excludes only unassigned major events from line comparisons.
6. **Rates and scale.** Service Delivered's raw field is 1/100 of the count ratio; standardized percentages use the actual/scheduled counts. Forty-two rows exceed 100% because actual counts exceed scheduled counts. They are not capped. Whether these reflect added trains or data corrections is not established by the source documentation.
7. **Incident counts are not severity or risk rates.** One incident may delay very different numbers of trains, and lines have different service exposure. Major incidents overlap conceptually with the broader incident dataset and use different categories; the two counts are never added together.
8. **Association is not causation.** Incident assignment involves dispatcher judgment, and categorization practices can change. Common disruptions, schedules, weather, demand, and operational changes may affect both incident counts and customer metrics. The monthly correlations in this project are descriptive, including the lagged check.
9. **Snapshot and revisions.** The baseline is the MTA portal extract recorded in `data/source_manifest.json` on 2026-11-19. MTA data can be revised. Re-download and rerun the pipeline before treating this as a current operational scorecard.
